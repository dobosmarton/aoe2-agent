# External AoE2:DE imagery for detector development

Research checked 2026-09-30. **Recommendation:** obtain later-game *recordings* through the official Age of Empires stats site and render them in the installed game, rather than scraping web screenshots. This gives varied match stages without asking the agent to survive to Castle/Imperial Age. Keep replay-derived and web-derived frames separate from a smaller, genuinely independent holdout captured through the agent's normal live-game path.

## Decision for the next image batch

First audit and deduplicate what we already have: the local `logs/` tree contains 1,464 image files, and `packages/detection/src/real_screenshots/raw` contains 220; those are file counts, not counts of independent or useful examples. Then **capture frames ourselves, but not necessarily play the matches ourselves**. The [official replay workflow](https://support.ageofempires.com/hc/en-us/articles/34920298779540-Downloading-Replay-Files-through-the-Age-of-Empires-Stats-page) lets us download recent matches from other players' stats pages and render their later-game moments in our installed client. That is the best balance of diverse, realistic scenes and visual consistency with the live detector. Check that each replay loads before scheduling annotation; the publisher warns that older-build recordings may not load. A replay view can still differ from the agent's live-player view, so sample and track those domains separately.

For gaps the replays do not cover, create targeted scenes in the [in-game Scenario Editor](https://www.ageofempires.com/news/age-of-empires-ii-definitive-edition-update-107882/) (for example, crowded cavalry/siege, occluded farms, and construction stages), or run AI games with a later [Starting Age](https://support.ageofempires.com/hc/en-us/articles/360047306372-How-do-I-create-a-multiplayer-match-in-Age-of-Empires-II-Definitive-Edition). These are fast ways to obtain native captures but are deliberately constructed distributions; use them for training and stress tests, not as the sole evidence of real-match performance. Reuse prior agent logs for failure mining, but hold out entire capture sessions from training and report metrics on fresh live-agent captures separately.

The pilot web-video frames and the 109-image Roboflow export are useful for visual inspection, not a ready-made training set: web video uses different UI/compression, while the export is heavily concentrated on near-identical Dark Age Town Center openings and has incomplete labels for our ontology. None removes the need for complete annotation. Keep source and rights metadata with every frame. [Microsoft's game-content rules](https://www.xbox.com/en-us/developers/rules) discuss screenshots and personal noncommercial derivative works, but do not explicitly grant model-training or dataset-publication rights; private collection is the conservative next step, and public release merits separate rights review.

## Rights boundary

[Microsoft's Game Content Usage Rules](https://www.xbox.com/en-us/developers/rules) cover gameplay footage and screenshots of qualifying Microsoft-published games, but grant a **limited, revocable, personal, noncommercial** use subject to conditions, including a required notice/link when sharing an Item. They do not explicitly settle whether extracting frames from someone else's replay/video for model training, or publishing a frame dataset or trained weights, is permitted. The [Microsoft copyright permissions page](https://www.microsoft.com/en-us/legal/intellectualproperty/copyright/permissions) likewise says copying and distributing content require permission from the applicable license/terms and points game-content questions to those rules. Therefore: document every source and its license, avoid publishing third-party frames or labels by default, and obtain a rights review or explicit permission before redistributing a corpus or making broader training-use claims. A contributor's CC label may cover their contribution without clearing the game's underlying IP. This is a risk assessment, not legal advice.

### Using YouTube gameplay specifically

| Activity | Practical boundary |
| --- | --- |
| Watch and shortlist scenes | [YouTube's Terms](https://www.youtube.com/t/terms) permit viewing for personal, noncommercial use. Record video URLs and timestamps as references; viewing does not grant a reusable copy. |
| Download or extract frames **from YouTube** | The same Terms restrict reproducing/downloading Content and automated access unless expressly authorized or prior written permission is obtained. [YouTube Help](https://support.google.com/youtube/answer/56100?hl=en) distinguishes downloading one's own uploads from watching someone else's offline in the app; the latter is not an extractable source file. The [API policies](https://developers.google.com/youtube/terms/developer-policies) separately prohibit API clients from downloading or storing audiovisual content without YouTube's prior written approval. Do not build a `yt-dlp`/scraping/frame-extraction pipeline as the default collection route. |
| Private training or evaluation | “Private” reduces redistribution exposure but does not itself provide permission to copy YouTube footage. [YouTube's user license](https://www.youtube.com/t/terms) enables playback/embeds, not independent reuse. If a creator supplies the **original file directly outside YouTube**, obtain written permission covering frame extraction, annotation, retention, and private model training/evaluation; assess the underlying game-content rights separately under [Microsoft's rules](https://www.xbox.com/en-us/developers/rules). |
| Publish a dataset, model, or card | Do not upload third-party video frames, extracted images, or annotated derivatives to Hugging Face without a separate rights review and adequate grants. A card can cite source URLs and report aggregate evaluation, but publishing trained weights also deserves review because neither platform's published terms explicitly address this use. [Microsoft's rules](https://www.xbox.com/en-us/developers/rules) set conditions on shared fan Items, including a notice and noncommercial limits. |

A video's **Standard YouTube license** is the default; a video's **CC BY** setting can permit reuse of the uploader's licensed contribution with attribution, and YouTube provides a [Creative Commons search filter](https://support.google.com/youtube/answer/2797468?hl=en). But [YouTube says](https://support.google.com/youtube/answer/2797468?hl=en) it cannot grant third-party rights, and [CC BY itself](https://creativecommons.org/licenses/by/4.0/) warns that other rights may still restrict use. For AoE2, the creator's license does not establish that they can relicense the underlying game art or grant an exception to YouTube's access terms. The cleanest YouTube-related route is to use it for discovery, contact a creator for **direct source footage** and a specific written ML-use grant, and then assess the game-content rights; otherwise prefer replay files rendered in our own client. Preserve the source file, permission record, game build, and match-level split in the dataset manifest. This is a conservative project policy, not a legal determination.

## Practical collection design

1. Use official replay downloads from distinct matches, not multiple adjacent frames from one match. Select map, civilization, age, player camera, and time ranges before labeling; record replay ID, game build, source URL, timestamp, capture settings, and a source-rights status. This is more useful for the main training batch than direct public screenshots or videos: it offers sustained later-game play rendered by the game engine, whereas web images are sparse/edited or have third-party platform and rights questions.
2. Capture sparse, unoverlaid frames in the VM at the agent's actual resolution. Prefer the native game renderer and live-player view where possible; mark spectator/replay UI as a separate domain. Deduplicate temporally and visually. Do not count sequential video frames as independent examples.
3. Annotate a *training/development* batch for the known gaps (food sources, farms, buildings, villagers, cavalry, crowded fights) and negatives. Check all in-scope visible objects and maintain the existing 60-class ontology rather than blindly importing external labels.
4. Keep a smaller untouched **live-agent** test set from separate sessions, even if these are opening-only at first. Add replay-rendered mid/late-game test strata held out by entire replay, clearly reported as a different domain. Do not tune thresholds or train on either test stratum.
5. Compare the retrained and current detector at the same preprocessing, thresholds, IoU, and runtime; report per-class precision/recall/F1 and support by source domain and game stage. A win on web/replay frames does not establish a win on the agent's camera view.

No media was downloaded or uploaded during this research.

## Existing capture utility

The repository already contains [`capture_replay.py`](../../packages/detection/src/extraction/capture_replay.py), which takes periodic `mss` screenshots of a selected **monitor** after the user manually opens a replay or game. It can be used for a small pilot now; it does not download or navigate replays. Before scaling collection, adapt it to crop the actual game window/agent viewport and attach replay or match ID, game timestamp/age, map, game build, settings, and source-rights metadata. Its default five-second fixed interval and sequential filenames do not prevent adjacent-frame leakage, and it does not filter menus or overlays. A manifest and stage-aware, sparse sampling should precede annotation. The current implementation also resolves its relative output against the script's package directory, so pass an explicit reviewed output path when using it.
